A reflection in three acts

What if it were you?

Nine decisive moments in the Bible — and the question no one who reads them carefully can shake.

The method

Each story, three questions.

For each passage we'll look at what came before, what happened in the moment, and what the consequences were. Then the invitation: if you were there, in the character's place — with the same fear, the same urgency, the same delay from God — what would you have done?

Act I · of 03
I.

Act one

Moses

The shepherd who became a deliverer. He met God in a bush, climbed a mountain, and came down to an impatient people. Four scenes: the call, the sea, the calf, the land he never set foot on.

I.1 · The call

Moses

Exodus 3:1-6

The bush that burned
but was not consumed.

Before

Moses was tending sheep in the Midian wilderness — a fugitive from Egypt, 40 years of quiet routine — when he saw a bush in flames that did not burn up.

After

God called him by name and sent him to deliver Israel. Moses covered his face and took off his sandals.

Consequences

There he received his life's mission. Removing his sandals was reverence — the ground was holy because God was on it.

I.1 · Reflection
“

What if it were you?

God calls you
to a mission
you didn't ask for.

Would you have removed your sandals — or argued there were more qualified people?

What, today, is your burning bush by the side of the road that you keep walking past?

I.2 · The sea

Moses

Exodus 14:21-31

Between Pharaoh
and a wall of water.

Before

Israel left Egypt and was cornered: the sea ahead, Pharaoh's army behind. Panic. At God's command, Moses stretched out his hand.

After

The sea opened. The people crossed on dry ground. The Egyptian army tried to follow the same path.

Consequences

The Egyptians died in the sea; Israel saw God's power and believed in the Lord — and in Moses as His messenger.

I.2 · Reflection
“

What if it were you?

No way out in sight,
and an entire people
looking at you.

Would you stretch out your hand over the sea — or keep looking for a more reasonable plan B?

What "Red Sea" stands in front of you right now, waiting on obedience before the miracle?

I.3 · The calf

Moses

Exodus 32:1-35

The leader delays.
The people improvise a god.

Before

Moses was on Sinai receiving the tablets. The people grew tired of waiting — forty days felt like a lifetime.

After

Aaron gave in, melted the gold, shaped a calf. The people worshiped. Moses came down, saw the festival, broke the tablets and destroyed the idol.

Consequences

About 3,000 men died that day. Severe judgment fell on the people for idolatry.

I.3 · Reflection
“

What if it were you?

God seems to delay.
Everyone wants
an answer now.

Would you be the Aaron — the one who caves to avoid conflict — or would you have held out the wait?

What "golden calves" do we melt down when God seems late?

I.4 · The land that wasn't

Moses

Numbers 14:1-35

One step away
from the promise.

Before

The twelve spies returned from Canaan. Ten brought a fearful report — giants, walls. The people began to grumble.

After

The people refused to trust God and wanted to return to Egypt. Only Joshua and Caleb spoke up to advance.

Consequences

That generation spent 40 years in the wilderness and never entered the land. Only Joshua and Caleb set foot in Canaan.

I.4 · Reflection
“

What if it were you?

The promise is right there.
But there are giants
in the way.

Would you have been with the majority who saw giants, or with the two who saw a God bigger than them?

What promises have you almost touched — and pulled back from at the last step?

Act II · of 03
II.

Act two

Noah

The man who heard the impossible and started hammering anyway. Three scenes: the absurd call, obedience against the crowd, and the man already saved who still stumbles.

II.1 · The call

Noah

Genesis 6:13-22; 7:1-5

"Build an ark
for a rain
no one has seen."

Before

The earth was corrupt and full of violence. In the middle of it, Noah found favor in God's eyes — a righteous man in his generation.

After

God explained the flood, gave the dimensions of the ark, told him to build. Noah obeyed — no protocol, no precedent.

Consequences

Noah and his family were preserved. He became an eternal example of obedience in the midst of chaos.

II.1 · Reflection
“

What if it were you?

God asks for something
nobody
will understand.

Would you start the work — or wait for the first cloud to be sure?

What's the "ark" God already told you to start, but you're still waiting on more signs?

II.2 · The challenges

Noah

Genesis 6:9-22; 7:1-24

Decades of work
under mockery.

Before

Noah lived in a corrupt generation and received an enormous task: build something nobody understood, for a long time, with no sign of rain.

After

He built the ark, faced disbelief all around him, and finally entered with his family — doors closed by God Himself.

Consequences

The flood came. God preserved Noah's household. After the waters, He sealed a new covenant with humanity.

II.2 · Reflection
“

What if it were you?

Years of work.
Zero validation.
Zero visible results.

Would you have persisted through the 30th time someone walked by and mocked the work?

Where, today, do you need to obey before understanding — or before seeing fruit?

II.3 · After the victory

Noah

Genesis 9:20-27

Saved from the flood,
stumbling in the peace.

Before

The flood was over. Noah went back to working the land — life starting again, routine reinstalled.

After

He planted a vineyard, drank the wine and got drunk. Ham saw his father's nakedness; Shem and Japheth covered him walking backwards, with respect.

Consequences

A curse fell on Canaan (Ham's son) and a blessing on Shem and Japheth. The episode exposes human fragility even after the miracle.

II.3 · Reflection
“

What if it were you?

After the crossing,
danger changes
addresses.

Would you have been Ham (exposing) or Shem and Japheth (covering) when seeing someone's weakness?

In what areas is your greatest risk not the storm — but the peace after it?

Act III · of 03
III.

Act three

Lot

The man who chose the green plain — and lost his ground. Two scenes: the escape from a doomed city, and what the ruins did to those who survived.

III.1 · The escape

Lot

Genesis 19:1-29

Leave now.
And don't look
back.

Before

Sodom was under imminent judgment. The angels arrived in the city and Lot received them, hosted them, protected them.

After

Lot left with his family in the morning, dragged by mercy. But his wife looked back — and became a pillar of salt.

Consequences

Sodom and Gomorrah were destroyed by fire and brimstone. Lot was saved — but he arrived on the other side with fewer people than he left with.

III.1 · Reflection
“

What if it were you?

Leaving is obedience.
Looking back
is still wanting to stay.

Would you have left right away, or kept negotiating one more day to "wrap things up"?

What "Sodom" does your heart still long for, even after God pulled you out of it?

III.2 · The isolation

Lot

Genesis 19:30-38

What trauma
and isolation
manufacture.

Before

Lot and his daughters lived isolated in a cave, fugitives, traumatized, after the destruction of Sodom.

After

The daughters made their father drunk on two consecutive nights and slept with him, convinced there were no men left on earth.

Consequences

Moab and Ben-Ammi were born — fathers of the Moabites and Ammonites, peoples who would clash with Israel for centuries. The family was marked by moral disorder.

III.2 · Reflection
“

What if it were you?

Surviving
is not the same
as being healed.

What do isolation, fear, and wine manufacture in someone who hasn't processed what they lived through?

Who have you let into your cave — or are you alone with God and people who only drink with you?

Synthesis · Consequences

The common thread

Every
choice
echoes.

Moses obeys — a sea opens and a people believes. The people doubt — an entire generation dies in the wilderness. Noah hammers through disbelief — a family survives the end of the world. Noah drinks his own wine — and a curse spans centuries. Lot hesitates — and loses his wife. Lot isolates himself — and the household manufactures its own ruin.

In every passage, obedience (or its absence) writes the next chapters for people not yet born.

Closing

One last question

What if it were you —
today?

Which of these stories hit you hardest? Which has the most to do with the week you're living right now — the bush, the sea, the delay, the giants, the ark, the vineyard, the escape, or the cave?
